* -1.1 to -0.4: Het Loss
* 0.3 to 1 gain
* 1+ amp

11.4 CIN calculation

CIN (Chromosomal instability) can be approximated by looking at the proportion of the genome/exome with copy number changes. We can calculate the degree of CIN by:

  • Considering the fraction of sampled genome with called CN which is not 2
  • Consider seg.mean FC greater than certain threshold (0.5)
  • Consider seg.mean FC greater than certain threshold (0.5) multiplied by purity

Depending on the threshold used, the adjustment may completely invert the correlation. Using called CN results in less biased distribution

# using the same metric
Cin3a=sapply(CNVlog2, function(x) CalcGGI(x$C-2, 0.1, x$loc.end-x$loc.start))
Cin3b=sapply(CNVlog2, function(x) CalcGGI(x$C-2, 0.1))
Cin1a=sapply(CNVlog2, function(x) CalcGGI(x$seg.mean, 0.5))
Cin3d=sapply(1:length(GeneCNVs), function(x) CalcGGI(CNVlog2[[x]]$seg.mean, 0.5*pinfo[["Purity", x]]))

colIds=SampleIDs$OutCol[match(names(Cin1a), SampleIDs$SAMPLE_ID)]
